### 2. CSL-FGMRES warm-start benchmark (afternoon)

Ran `experiments/warmstart_v2/03_csl_fgmres_eval.py` for pair **16→32**, comparing:
- **Z**: zero-start (cold FGMRES with CSL preconditioner)
- **W (net)**: neural warm-start, direct prediction
- **W (alpha_residual_net)**: neural warm-start rescaled by a residual-correction factor

**5 test samples, 60 FGMRES iterations, beta=0.3 CSL:**

| Iteration | Zero-start rel-res | Neural (net) rel-res |
|----------:|-------------------:|---------------------:|
| k=10      | 0.696              | 0.341 (2.0× lower)   |
| k=20      | 0.133              | 0.066 (2.0× lower)   |
| k=40      | 0.0021             | 0.0011 (1.9× lower)  |
| k=60      | 1.76e-5            | 8.4e-6 (2.1× lower)  |

**Threshold iteration costs:**
- Reach 0.1: zero needs **40**, neural needs **20** (2× saving)
- Reach 0.01: zero needs **40**, neural needs **40** (no saving at this threshold)
- Reach 0.001: zero needs **60**, neural needs **56** (marginal)

**Alpha-residual variant** (residual-correction rescaling): essentially no improvement over zero-start — r0_warm ≈ 1.0, trajectories nearly identical. The rescaling strategy does not work.

**Interpretation:** The neural warm-start consistently provides ~2× reduction in residual at any fixed iteration count, but in absolute terms both arrive at full convergence by ~60 iterations. The practical win is at loose-tolerance regimes (e.g. target 0.1 for a multigrid smoother) where the saving is real and substantial.
